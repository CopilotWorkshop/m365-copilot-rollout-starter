# Starter prompts for a Copilot pilot

These are starting points, not official Microsoft prompts. Change the bracketed parts to match your work. Before you share any answer, run the check under the prompt. Microsoft's free documentation and Prompt Gallery remain the place to learn official product behavior.

If you only have Copilot Chat, paste or attach the relevant text or file into the chat. If a prompt needs mail, calendar, or a file in an app you do not have Copilot in, use the same wording in Copilot Chat with the content pasted in.

## 1. Define the pilot in one page

**Prompt:** "Turn these notes into a one-page pilot brief for Microsoft 365 Copilot. Use four headings: who is in the pilot, what a useful result looks like, what is out of scope, and when we review a go / hold / stop decision. Flag anything in the notes that is vague or missing. Notes: [paste]"

**Use it when:** You have messy kickoff notes and need a brief a sponsor can actually review.

**Check:** Every heading has a named owner or a clearly marked gap. Do not publish a brief that invents a scope you did not agree.

## 2. Turn a procedure into a checklist

**Prompt:** "Convert this procedure into a numbered checklist a colleague could follow without me in the room. Keep the original order. Mark any step that is ambiguous or missing an owner. Procedure: [paste]"

**Use it when:** You are writing a short how-we-work note for the pilot group.

**Check:** Each step is an action. If the source was unclear, the output should say so rather than guessing.

## 3. Recap a meeting for the decision log

**Prompt:** "From this transcript or notes, list: decisions, open questions, owners, and due dates. Use only what is in the text. If a decision or owner was not stated, say 'not stated' instead of filling it in. Text: [paste]"

**Use it when:** You need a clean record for the go / hold / stop review.

**Check:** Compare every decision and owner against the source. Remove anything the model inferred.

## 4. Draft a short pilot announcement

**Prompt:** "Draft a 150-word email to our Copilot pilot group. Cover: why they were chosen, one example of a useful task, what not to put into Copilot, and who to ask for help. Use a plain, direct tone. Audience: [team]. Example task: [example]. What to avoid: [rule]. Help contact: [name]."

**Use it when:** You need a first draft of the launch note.

**Check:** The "what not to put into Copilot" line matches your actual rule. The contact is a real person.

## 5. Stress-test an acceptable-use question

**Prompt:** "A colleague asks: '[question]'. Based only on this policy excerpt, answer 'allowed', 'not allowed', or 'needs a human decision', and quote the line you used. If the excerpt does not cover the question, say so. Excerpt: [paste]"

**Use it when:** You want a first pass before a human owner answers.

**Check:** If the excerpt is silent, the answer must be "needs a human decision." Do not let the model invent a policy.

## 6. Find contradictions in a draft plan

**Prompt:** "Read this draft rollout plan. List contradictions, missing owners, and dates that do not line up. Do not rewrite the plan. Quote the conflicting lines. Draft: [paste]"

**Use it when:** Two people have edited the same plan.

**Check:** Each item cites a line in the draft. Drop anything that is just a style preference.

## 7. Build a RAID list from notes

**Prompt:** "From these project notes, draft a RAID table (risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies) with five starter rows total, each with an owner and a review date. Mark rows that are inferred rather than stated. Notes: [paste]"

**Use it when:** You need a starting log for the pilot, not a final risk register.

**Check:** Inferred rows stay labeled. Owners who were not in the notes must be blank, not invented.

## 8. Check a spreadsheet summary

**Prompt:** "Summarize this table: totals by [category], the top five rows by [measure], and any totals that do not add up. Point out missing labels or mixed units. Do not recommend a business decision. Data: [paste or attach]"

**Use it when:** You are checking a pilot metric or a practice file.

**Check:** Recalculate the top figures. If the source is messy, the summary should say so.

## 9. Rewrite for a new reader

**Prompt:** "Rewrite this update for [audience: executives / IT / the pilot group]. Keep it under 200 words. Keep every fact that is in the source. Put unanswered questions at the end. Do not add benefits or results that are not in the source. Source: [paste]"

**Use it when:** The same update has to go to more than one group.

**Check:** No new metrics, quotes, or claims appear. Questions stay questions.

## 10. Ask Copilot to check its own answer

**Prompt:** "Review your last answer. List: (1) claims that are directly supported by the source I provided, (2) claims that were inferred, and (3) anything that should be verified by a person before sharing. Do not strengthen the original answer."

**Use it when:** You are about to paste a Copilot result into an email or deck.

**Check:** Treat inferred items as unverified. A person still does the final read.
